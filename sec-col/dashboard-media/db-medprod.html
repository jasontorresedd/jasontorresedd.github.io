Learning & Media Design Operations

Faster, leaner online course development: 5 years of multimedia production

From Fall 2020 to Spring 2025, I led a central team of media producers (MPs) and provided guidance to a team of instructional designers (IDs) in the development multimedia for online courses. Over those years, team member work per course fell from to hours () and cost per course fell from to (). A design phase was established to steer effort toward needs for effective instruction in recorded media and post-production, recording moved into the studio, and producing a mix of videos and podcasts was embraced. Additionally, new vendors were selected for stock assets, new project management software was adapted, and later AI tools were piloted.

Appendix

These notes give background for reading the dashboard. They will help you to understand what was happening during the five years, how the course development workflow ran, and how each number was calculated.

Context of the work

  • Work-from-home period (July 2020 to August 2021). All course design and development, including media production, was remote. Faculty recorded in their homes and uploaded to cloud storage; MPs downloaded and edited at home. Nothing was done in person. A new data capture strategy and integrated course development workflow followed in Fall 2021.
  • Design time was estimated. Phase 2 design ranged from a couple of hours to a couple of days. The workflow called for two design meetings, but sometimes only one was needed.
  • The move to podcasts was intentional. Podcasts took less time to produce, and faculty reported that students asked for them after the COVID-19 pandemic.
  • Targeted production speed was 30 minutes of MP work per 1 minute of final video, and 5 minutes of work per 1 minute of final podcast audio.
  • Spring 2025 data is incomplete. I stopped collecting data with a few weeks left in the term because I was laid off.

Integrated course development workflow

A shared workflow between instructional designers and multimedia producers. Go to integrated workflow to learn more.

  1. Phase 1 Project Initiation: collection, analysis, and conceptualization
  2. Phase 2 Design: IDs lead course design; MPs lead multimedia design
  3. Phase 3a Production: MPs record and produce
  4. Phase 3b Development: IDs build the course in the LMS
  5. Phase 4 Evaluation

How the numbers are calculated

  • Source. A spreadsheet production tracker that includes: projects, of which were canceled. Canceled projects are left out of every chart.
  • Academic years run Fall to Summer, so 2021–22 is FA21, SP22 and SU22. Projects are placed by the term they started. Summer projects appear as a single SU term.
  • MP hours add Phase 2 design hours (weeks × estimated hours per week), Phase 3 studio hours, and Phase 3 post–production work time. Design hours and studio time were not tracked before Fall 2021.
  • Work per final minute divides MP post-production work time by final media minutes. Group values are pooled (total work ÷ total minutes). Per–course values use medians.
  • Lead producer is the MP who recorded and produced the media. Before Fall 2021 that role wasn't logged, so the primary production MP is used instead.
  • Costs follow the tracker's formula: design hours × project manager rate, studio hours × recording MP rate, and production hours (rounded up to the quarter hour) × the average rate of the production MPs, plus $100 materials. From Spring 2024 on, the tracker left production hours blank, and Spring 2025 has no rates. Those courses are rebuilt with the same formula using recorded work time and each MP's most recent rate. They're marked est. in tooltips and the project table. When an ID served as project manager, no rate exists, so that design time isn't costed.
  • Dates. Before Fall 2021, end dates were mostly logged as the first of a month, so early durations are approximate. One Fall 2021 end date logged as 1/26/21 is read as 1/26/22, which matches its recorded 40 days past target.
  • Videos are counted only when final minutes were recorded. One Spring 2022 course lists 7 videos with no minutes or work time.
