AI in Learning Design

GA · 2026

A description of how I have incorporated Artificial Intelligence (AI) into my learning design process. While I do not offload writing content, activities, assessments, and other aspects of a learning experience, I use Anthropic Claude as my design partner and Google Gemini AI Pro as part of research, technical validation, and occasional image generation.

Overview

The use of AI in learning design should not be done in the pursuit of replacing human effort and imagination. When it comes to creating a learning experience, decisions must reside with humans. This applies to the application of learning theory, creating ways for learners to engage with content, designing situations for learners to interact with others, building assessments, and ultimately mapping out the integration of all the components that make a learning experience. That said, I do not apply AI or advise anyone else to turn to AI and ask it to create on its own any part of a learning experience. Instead, my approach to using AI in learning design, and work for that matter, is to do the same thinking work I have always done. Through that process, I write all of the materials, and create either full versions or distilled artifacts that I can share with AI to serve as context, constraints, guidelines, and as a roadmap for what I aim to achieve.

Of the various frontier AI models, my choice is to use Anthropic Claude — primarily the Opus model. Within the desktop application, I will create a project for all learning design. (I do not create individual projects for each training or course project I am developing.) I write instructions for this overarching project and share a series of Markdown files that serve as core context. For example, these are the rules and guidelines I need to follow, project workflows, and frameworks I like to reference as part of my expertise and experience. I then add project and client specific files (in Markdown) that are my notes, drafted background information, and design files that I am working on. How I use this project then is as a project management and design partner that can help me recall past decisions, identify interlinkages and inconsistencies, recall reusable content, and act as a design partner when ideating possibilities. Beyond the project, I will create desktop folders as needed and drop in templates and my design plan, to then help me quickly create drafts of common files I need to share.

In short, my approach to AI in learning design is not to have AI do the thinking or learning design, but rather leverage it to speed up the processes and workflows of creating documentation from my design outputs, help me to identify misalignments in my documented ideas, and generate on occasion options when I hit roadblocks in my thinking or need additional information. For me, AI is my partner and not my stand-in.

Design process

There are a few core design documents for communicating with internal and external stakeholders. These are the High-Level Design (HLD) slide deck, the Discovery-Design-Development (DDD) slide deck, and the Competency Map. Pulling these together for presentation can take a lot of time and opens the door for some inconsistency in communicating the intended design. In particular, this can happen if memory fails or new ideas creep in. A very human thing. While not a 1:1 match, I created Markdown template versions that include key parts in the slide decks. I then work with Claude to make initial drafts from my design plans. Additionally, I created with Claude an HTML version of a competency map that I also use to create a draft from my design plans. In every case, these are starting points that I read through and revise ensuring alignment with plans before sharing.

Impact

In my case, using AI to speed up the processes and workflows of creating documentation from my design outputs has been very successful and opened the door for me to use more time for meetings, coaching, planning, and development work. While I have not quantified it, I know from past experience that hours of time have been saved, and through the way I have used AI, it has prevented me from introducing inconsistencies in documentation. As noted before, I refuse to use AI to draft full documents and slide decks. The temptation is there and it would be easy to try given the plans I provide and discussions I have with Claude. But I know it would create more work. I have seen this happen repeatedly with others who use AI to create documents and full slide decks. And each time they end up spending just as much time correcting them as they would building them. So at the most, what I have done and recommend is to ask AI to draft a slide outline, discuss and iterate, and then manually build on the joint outline.

Overall, integrating AI into learning design is an ongoing and developing process for me that is no different from the clients' own. These people and teams too are figuring out how to integrate AI tools into what they do. Nonetheless, using AI has been helpful to answer questions on process, initially draft materials, generate simple visuals, and occasionally discuss and refine design decisions. In particular, AI has been most useful when learning and planning for content outside of my area of expertise. In these cases, I have found AI very useful for topic research and to find other examples of related instruction and training.

Going forward, my plan is to continue refining how I use AI as a partner in my work and learning design practice. I intentionally remain metacognitively aware of how I am processing collaboration and when I am offloading my cognitive effort. Admittedly, I have caught myself offloading a few times and end up taking a step back. Sometimes I have thought to myself, “why do you lean on this when you know what to do”. But then soon after multiple requests happen, deadlines are on the horizon, and leaning on AI is the best way to get to ‘done’. So to ensure that ‘done’ is good, I have focused on instructions, skills, context, and templates in optimal formats (e.g., Markdown, CSV, HTML) to improve the effectiveness of my communication and collaboration with AI. I believe I will find greater and greater impact.
