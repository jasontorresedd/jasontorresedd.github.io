AI in Learning Design

GA · 2026

A description of how I have incorporated Artificial Intelligence (AI) into my learning design process. I use Anthropic Claude for workflows and documents, and Google Gemini Pro as a content and technical researcher. I do not use them, though, to write actual content, activities, assessments, and other aspects of a learning experience.

Overview

I believe the use of AI in learning design should done to enhance human skills and imagination. Only humans can create a learning experience that speaks to humans. Trained educators remain the best options for applying learning theory, creating content engagement, designing opportunities for interaction, designing assessments, and ultimately telling storyies and drafting media that connects to human desires and emotion.

When I use AI, I lean on it as a thinking partner, a development assistant, and an expert coder following instructions and style guides I have written. I purposefully plan to not let AI replace my thinking. In practice, use AI to help me build materials based on my own work. I do not look to it to work autonomously and create any part of learning content, media, or a story, from its own imagination or predictions. Instead, I want my AI partner(s) to produce outputs using context, constraints, guidelines, and roadmaps that I layout. But occassionally, even I need a little help seeing things differently. So I'll ask AI to surprise me.

My preference is to use Anthropic Claude — primarily the Opus model. I use this through the desktop application and within VS Code. Using the desktop app, my process is to create a single project for all learning design. I write and update instructions for this project and share files for context that include the following: rules and guidelines I need to follow, project workflows, project notes, and frameworks I like to use. I also add notes on the project and client that cover background information and instructional plans that I am working on. (I provide all of these in Markdown format.) I then use this project as a project management and design partner that can help me keep alignment, recall past decisions, identify inconsistencies, recall previous ideas and content, and act as a rapid prototyper for all kinds of materials.

While I have effectively changed my operating system since early 2023 working with AI, some things remain fundamentally the same. I still research, study, draw, and write all of the base content of a course, lesson, or training. The difference now, is that I create these in a way that can easily be shared with AI and I turn to AI to help me produce outputs quicker.

Discovery process

Every project begins with a needs analysis, and this typically involves interviewing stakeholders. These are done with decision-makers, technical experts, exemplary staff members, and/or potential learners. To help get a head start planning these interviews, I shared with Claude some of my notes and questions I like to ask interviewees. After some back-and-forth, we created a reusable question bank. You may use this as well as a starting point.

Design process

The design process involves the creation of four core documents: 1) the High-Level Training Program Design, 2) the Competency Map, 3) the High-Level Design (HLD) slide deck, and 4) the Discovery-Design-Development (DDD) slide deck. Items 1 and 2 are critical to communicating the design to stakeholders and collaborating with Claude. And items 3 and 4 are part of communicating the direction and story of the training to the client team. My practice is to write the full High-Level Training Program Design myself, then use Claude to create drafts of the other 3 items that I revise and edit going into final versions. I fill out each template and share them with Claude during the design process.

Impact

Using AI to speed up the process of creating documentation has been the most impactful aspect of using AI in learning design for me. But for this to work, I still put in the time to write a full program design plan for each project, then use that document as the foundation for everything that follows. With each project, I look for opportunities to expand partnering with Claude in ways that I feel comfortable with and can trust. So far though, the time AI has saved has allowed me more time for people, planning, thinking, and even rest. This has worked because its outputs are based on my thinking work that I document, structure, and share.

Integrating AI into my work and learning design is an ongoing and developing process for me. I am intentionally taking steps and iterating on those as I see it evolve and monitor how much I allow it to take on. At the same time, I am very careful to avoid cognitive offloading. Nonetheless, I have found AI quite helpful in my work and particularly in the following ways:

  • answering questions on process;
  • drafting initial materials;
  • converting images from PNG to SVG for use in Markdown;
  • generating simple visuals;
  • researching content outside of my expertise;
  • rapidly building folder/file structures for distribution;
  • discussing learning activity ideas based on my notes; and
  • quickly checking spelling, grammar, and content alignment across documents; and
  • generating DOCX files consistent with layout in Markdown files.
