Learning and Media Operations

GWU · 2020–2025

An outline of work done as part of transforming an existing online course design and development process for improved operational efficiency, sustainability, teaming, and quality learning and multimedia deliverables.

Overview

Upon joining GWU as the inaugural Director of Strategic Digital Learning Initiatives I had two tasks: 1) create digital fluency and later AI programs and 2) take over an existing multimedia production team using their skills to teach and enable digital skills among faculty. I joined at a time of severe fear, worry, and separation with the COVID pandemic. So I observed, coached, and managed the first year, then working with a colleague, began to transform learning and multimedia production operations impacting a team of Instructional Designers and Multimedia Producers when we returned in-person during 2021.

I determined that if staff were to contribute to digital initiatives beyond online course development, then they needed time to do that work and opportunities to upskill. The current system had my team members taking orders from everyone, Instructional Designers going through unconstrained rounds of changes, and each project taking 9-10 months to complete. Getting to a place where my team could have the impact desired from leadership meant rewriting workflows, re-defining how teams engaged, outlining team member responsiblities, allowing team members to apply their expertise with clients, setting constraints on deliverables, and formalizing the daily use of digital project boards and trackers.

Impact

The result of this work was tremendous. By the time I left GWU, a regular batch of 9-13 online course developments were being consistently delivered within 4-5 months. Within one term a course could be developed and launched the next term. Additionally, multimedia production costs were down 50%. Team members were client-facing, able to contribute to strategic digital initiatives, able to attend trainings and conferences, and importantly could take time for themselves and remain healthy. The last one was a big concern for me as I never seen people so burned out when I arrived.

But it didn't go without push-back. Change is hard for many and someone with my mixed background is sometimes seen as too business by some in higher-ed and too academic by some in the private sector. I made changes and policies where I could. I routinely coached team members on what they could do and how to do it. And I made a habit of documenting and communicating all changes along the way. As I left, I was touched by a team member calling me and telling me, I've really hoped for and looked for a manager like you.
